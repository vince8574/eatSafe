One pepper, dozens of brands: the jalapeño Salmonella recall spreading across US stores

FDA USDA Salmonella Published August 22, 2026

Between August 8 and 12, 2026, one supplier's fresh jalapeños set off recalls at Whole Foods, Trader Joe's, Target, Kroger, Hannaford, Stop & Shop and HEB — plus a nationwide USDA public health alert. Almost none of those packages mention a jalapeño supplier.

According to the company announcements published by the FDA, Coast Citrus Distributors recalled fresh jalapeños over possible Salmonella contamination. The FDA and CDC state that they are, with state and local partners, investigating illnesses in a multistate Salmonella outbreak linked to those peppers.

What happened next is the part worth understanding. The peppers had already been chopped into other companies' finished products:

  • August 8 — NatureBest Precut & Produce recalls pico de gallo, stuffed mushrooms and soup mixes sold under the NatureBest and HEB names.
  • August 9 — Taylor Fresh Foods recalls finished dips, salsas and guacamole sold as Trader Joe's, Freshness Guaranteed, Private Selection and unbranded store products at Target, Kroger, Hannaford and Stop & Shop.
  • August 11 — Salata Dressings recalls its Jalapeno Avocado Dressing, sold in HEB stores in Texas.
  • August 12 — Whole Foods Market recalls select salsas, guacamole, pico de gallo and prepared foods.
  • August 12 — Hardie's Fresh Foods recalls 5 lb cases of jalapeños in Texas.

The USDA says the list is not finished

On August 8, USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service issued a nationwide public health alert covering meat and poultry products made with the same peppers. Its wording is unusually direct:

"FSIS expects additional downstream products will be identified as this ingredient recall progresses. As more information becomes available, FSIS will update this public health alert. Consumers should check back frequently."

In other words, the agency itself does not yet know the full extent. An ingredient recall unfolds backwards: the supplier is identified first, then every manufacturer that bought from them, then every retailer that sold the result. Each step adds names — and none of those names appeared on the original recall.

Why this one is hard to catch at home

Three things make this recall unusually difficult for a shopper.

The brand on the package is not the company that recalled. Coast Citrus Distributors does not appear on a tub of guacamole. Neither does Taylor Fresh Foods, on most of it — the label says Trader Joe's, or Private Selection, or nothing at all. Several rows in the FDA's own product table list the brand as "NONE".

There is no lot number. The recalled items are identified by a "Best if Used By" date range and a UPC — for instance a Kroger Spicy Pimento Cheese Dip covering 8/07/2026 through 8/15/2026. If you go looking for a lot code, you will not find one.

The products are eaten cold. Salsa, guacamole, pico de gallo and dips get no cooking step. Whatever is in them reaches the plate.

Salmonella, briefly

Symptoms usually appear 6 hours to 6 days after eating: fever, diarrhea that may be bloody, nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps. Most healthy adults recover within a week. The FDA notices single out young children, elderly and frail people, and anyone with a weakened immune system, in whom the bacteria can enter the bloodstream and cause severe complications such as infected aneurysms, endocarditis and arthritis.

Whole Foods reported no illnesses connected to its own products at the time of its announcement — but the wider outbreak investigation is what set the entire chain in motion.

What to do

Check your refrigerator for salsa, guacamole, pico de gallo, dips, prepared sandwiches, burritos and salad kits bought since early August. Match the UPC and the "Best if Used By" date against the official notices — not the brand alone, since the brand is often the store's own. If it matches, or if you are unsure, do not eat it: return it for a refund or discard it. Wash anything the product touched, including hands, containers and shelves. If you develop a fever with diarrhea in the following days, contact a healthcare provider and mention the product — especially for a young child or a vulnerable adult.
