A Class I Recall for Undeclared Fish — Identified Only by a Best-By Date

FDA Class I Allergens Published August 4, 2026

A vegetarian-sounding product that may contain fish is exactly the kind of recall that catches people out. This one also illustrates something we keep running into: there is no lot number to check.

According to the FDA's enforcement report, Gellert Global Group recalled "Fusia, Asian Inspirations, Kimchi & Tofu Kimbap", 8.1 oz (230 g), for an undeclared allergen: fish. The FDA classified it Class I — its most serious tier, meaning a reasonable probability of serious harm or death.

Why "kimchi and tofu" can still contain fish

This isn't a strange manufacturing accident; it's how the food is traditionally made. Kimchi very often contains fish — salted shrimp (jeotgal), anchovy sauce or fish sauce are standard in many recipes, used to drive the fermentation and the savoury depth. A shopper reading "Kimchi & Tofu" reasonably reads it as plant-based. Someone with a fish allergy might pick it up precisely because it looks like the safe option.

That's what makes an undeclared allergen a Class I hazard rather than paperwork. The product is not contaminated in any visible way — the label simply fails to warn the one person who needed the warning.

The identifier is a date, not a lot

Here's the detail worth pausing on. The FDA notice identifies the affected product with a single piece of information: "Best if Used By: October 8, 2027". No lot code, no batch number.

This is far more common than people expect. Fresh items, chilled ready-to-eat foods and many store brands carry no lot number at all, so the recall notice falls back on the date stamp — sometimes a single date, sometimes a range. It's official, it's precise, and it's nearly useless if you don't happen to have the federal notice open while standing at your fridge. Comparing a date on a package against a published recall window is exactly the kind of check a phone should do for you.

What to do

If you have Fusia Kimchi & Tofu Kimbap and anyone in your household is allergic to fish, check the Best if Used By date against the FDA notice and do not eat it if it matches — return it or throw it out. If you are unsure, treat it as affected: an undeclared allergen reaction can be severe and fast. If someone reacts after eating it, treat it as an allergy emergency and seek care.
