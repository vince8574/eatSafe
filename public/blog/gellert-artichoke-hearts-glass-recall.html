Glass in the Artichoke Hearts: a Recall You'd Only Meet at a Restaurant

FDAForeign materialClass II Published July 31, 2026

Not every recall is something you'd find in your own pantry. Some pass through kitchens you never see — and this is one of them.

According to the FDA's enforcement report, Gellert Global Group (New Jersey) recalled "Ruby, Artichoke Hearts Quartered" in a foodservice-size can (5 lb 8 oz / 2,500 g, drained weight ~3 lb) because "foreign material (glass) was found in the product." The FDA classified it Class II, and the recall was listed as ongoing.

What "Class II" means here

The FDA sorts recalls into three tiers. Class I is the most serious (reasonable probability of serious harm or death). Class II — the tier for this one — means a situation where use may cause temporary or medically reversible adverse health consequences, or where the probability of serious harm is remote. That's not "harmless": glass can still cut or injure. The classification reflects the assessed likelihood and severity, not a promise that nothing can go wrong.

The food you never bought

The tell here is the package size. A 5 lb 8 oz can isn't a grocery item — it's a foodservice format, the kind that goes to restaurants, caterers and institutional kitchens. So most people will never see this recall on a shelf; they'd only ever encounter the product on a plate, with no label to check. It's a quiet reminder that a lot of what we eat is prepared from ingredients we never handle — and that recall information, however official, only helps when it can reach the point where the food is actually served.

What to do

If you run or supply a kitchen and stock "Ruby" quartered artichoke hearts in the foodservice can, check the brand, size and lot code against the FDA notice and pull any matching stock from use. For everyday shoppers there's no consumer package to check here — but it's a good moment to remember that "foreign material" recalls are common, and a quick barcode check is worth it on any product you can scan.
