Two Deaths Are Now Linked to the Iceberg Lettuce Cyclospora Outbreak

CDC Cyclospora Taco Bell Published August 4, 2026

When we first covered this outbreak, the official tally listed no deaths. That has changed. The CDC now says it is aware of two cyclosporiasis-related deaths — and the case count has climbed past 1,900.

This is an update to our earlier coverage: the recall that had no lot number and the Taco Bell link and the false positive.

Where the outbreak stands

According to the CDC, the total case count is now 1,947, with illnesses reported from nine states: Illinois, Indiana, Kansas, Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania and West Virginia. At least 98 people have been hospitalized.

And the part that changed: the CDC states it is aware of two cyclosporiasis-related deaths in Michigan, in people with underlying health conditions. It's worth being precise here, because the agency itself is: those deaths are not counted in the laboratory-confirmed case tally the CDC publishes. They are reported as related to the outbreak.

Why deaths are notable for this parasite

Cyclospora cayetanensis is rarely fatal. It causes prolonged watery diarrhea, cramping and fatigue that can drag on for weeks and relapse — miserable, but usually survivable in a healthy adult. Deaths point to the same pattern seen with most foodborne pathogens: the danger concentrates in people whose bodies have less margin. Prolonged diarrhoea means dehydration and electrolyte loss, and for someone already managing a serious condition, that can be enough to tip the balance.

It's the reason "it's just a stomach bug" is a poor way to think about foodborne illness. The same parasite that gives one person a bad fortnight can be dangerous to the person next to them.

The source, and a common mix-up

The implicated product is iceberg lettuce sourced from central Mexico, recalled by Taylor Farms de México, which announced on July 17, 2026 that it was voluntarily removing all of it from the U.S. market. The restaurant chain identified in the CDC and FDA investigation is Taco Bell — shredded iceberg served at locations in several states.

Worth stating plainly, because the two get confused: this outbreak is not connected to Chipotle. Chipotle had a well-publicised E. coli outbreak back in 2015, and the word "chipotle" also shows up in unrelated recalled product names (seasonings, salad kits). Neither has anything to do with the 2026 Cyclospora investigation.

What to do

If you ate at an affected location during the exposure window and have had watery diarrhea lasting more than a few days, cramping and fatigue, contact a healthcare provider and mention Cyclospora specifically — routine stool panels don't always test for it unless asked. Symptoms can relapse, so a partial recovery isn't proof you're clear. For anyone with a compromised immune system or a serious underlying condition, don't wait it out.
