Reser's Pasta Salad Recall: When the Label Is the Hazard

USDA / FSISAllergens Published June 25, 2026

A tub labeled "pasta salad" may actually contain chicken salad — with egg and milk that never made it onto the label. For someone with a food allergy, a wrong label isn't a paperwork error. It's the danger itself.

On June 25, 2026, the USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) announced that Reser's Fine Foods, Inc., a Halifax, North Carolina establishment, is recalling approximately 5,300 pounds of a ready-to-eat product labeled as pasta salad, due to misbranding and undeclared allergens. FSIS classified it as a Class I recall.

What happened

Per the FSIS notice, the product labeled as pasta salad may actually contain chicken salad, which contains egg and milk — known allergens that are not declared on the label. The affected product was produced on June 11, 2026:

  • 5-lb plastic tubs of "Molly's Kitchen California Style Pasta Salad", with "USE BY JUL/16/26 430" printed on the side of the tub.

Why a labeling mistake is a Class I recall

For most shoppers, a mislabeled tub is a curiosity. For someone allergic to egg or milk, it is a serious hazard: they read the label, see nothing that warns them, and eat a product that can trigger a reaction. That is exactly why undeclared allergens are one of the most common reasons for recalls in the United States, and why FSIS treats them as Class I — "a reasonable probability of serious, adverse health consequences."

It also shows a limit of reading the label alone. The label was there; it was simply wrong. The safety net that catches this kind of error is the official recall notice — which is only useful if the person holding the tub knows it exists and can match it to what's in their hand.

What to do

If you have a 5-lb tub of "Molly's Kitchen California Style Pasta Salad" with "USE BY JUL/16/26 430," and anyone in your household is allergic to egg or milk, do not eat it — return it or throw it out. If you've had an allergic reaction after eating it, contact a healthcare provider. Others can discard it out of caution.
