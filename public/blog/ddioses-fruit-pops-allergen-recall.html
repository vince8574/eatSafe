A Fruit Pop That May Hide Milk, Pecans and Pistachios — a Class I Recall

FDAAllergensClass I Published July 31, 2026

A frozen treat marketed as a fruit pop shouldn't contain milk, pecans or pistachios. When one might, and the label doesn't say so, the FDA calls it a Class I recall — the most serious tier there is.

In late July 2026, D'Dioses Fruit Pops, Inc. (New Jersey) recalled a broad line of its ice pops because they "may contain undeclared milk, pecans, pistachios, yellow #5 and red #40." The recall spans many flavors — Nuez, Tamarindo, Arroz con Leche, Fresa Leche, Mango, Limón, Cookies & Cream, Pistachio, Piña Colada and Nanche among them — in bars and cups from roughly 3.7 oz to 4 oz. The FDA classified it Class I.

Why an "allergen" recall is the most serious kind

It's tempting to read "labeling issue" as minor. It isn't. The FDA defines Class I as a situation with a reasonable probability that use will cause serious adverse health consequences or death. For someone with a milk or tree-nut allergy, an undeclared allergen fits that definition exactly: a few bites of what they believed was a safe fruit pop can trigger anaphylaxis. The hazard here isn't a pathogen — it's the gap between what the label says and what's in the bar.

The undeclared dyes (FD&C Yellow #5 / tartrazine and Red #40) matter for a smaller group who react to them, but the milk and nuts are what push this into Class I territory.

How to check what's in your freezer

Allergen recalls are, in one sense, easier to verify than a produce outbreak: the affected products carry a UPC barcode, and the FDA notice lists them. For example, the "Nuez" bar is UPC 827912008548, "Tamarindo" is 827912008593, and "Arroz con Leche" is 710594511843. If you have D'Dioses pops, the reliable move is to match the UPC on the wrapper against the recall list — not the flavor name or the look of the bar.

That is precisely the kind of check a phone is good at and a tired shopper is not. Scanning the barcode turns "is this the recalled one?" from a squint-and-guess into a yes/no answer.

What to do

If you have D'Dioses ice pops and anyone in your household is allergic to milk or tree nuts, don't eat them until you've matched the UPC to the recall — and when in doubt, discard or return them. An allergic reaction to an undeclared allergen can be severe and fast; if someone reacts after eating one, treat it as you would any allergy emergency and seek care.
