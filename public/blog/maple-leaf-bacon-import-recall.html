USDA Class I Recall: 12,000 lb of Maple Leaf Bacon Imported Without Reinspection

USDA / FSISClass I Published July 24, 2026

A Class I recall doesn't always mean the food is contaminated. Sometimes it means the paperwork that proves it's safe was never completed — and that's exactly what happened here.

On July 24, 2026, the USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) announced that Maple Leaf Foods, Inc., of Lisle, Illinois, is recalling approximately 12,036 pounds of not-ready-to-eat (NRTE) smoked bacon that was imported from Canada without the benefit of import reinspection into the United States. FSIS classified it as a Class I recall — its highest tier.

What's affected

Per the FSIS notice, the refrigerated smoked bacon was produced on June 9, 10, 12, 13, and 15, 2026, and includes:

  • 12-oz vacuum packages of "Royale Natural Applewood Smoked ALL NATURAL Uncured Bacon," Product of Canada, with sell-by dates of SEP 01 2026 and SEP 07 2026 printed on the side of the package;
  • 12-oz vacuum packages of "TOP VALU Uncured Hardwood Smoked Bacon," Product of Canada, with various September sell-by dates.

The full label images and complete product list are on the FSIS notice linked below.

Why "Class I" here doesn't mean "contaminated"

This is the part worth understanding. FSIS reserves Class I for situations with "a reasonable probability that the use of the product will cause serious, adverse health consequences." That usually signals a pathogen or a dangerous foreign object. Here, the reason is different: the product crossed the border without the mandatory U.S. import reinspection.

Import reinspection is the checkpoint where FSIS verifies that imported meat meets U.S. standards. When product skips it, the agency has no record confirming it's safe — so it treats the product as potentially unsafe by default. There is no confirmed contamination in this recall and, as is typical for these cases, no reported illnesses. But because the safety verification never happened, the precautionary classification is high.

It's the same logic behind a recall with no lot number, or a "positive" lab sample that later gets walked back: the absence of proof is not proof of absence. A missing inspection stamp is treated as a real risk, not a technicality.

What to do

Check your fridge and freezer for "Royale" Applewood Smoked or "TOP VALU" Hardwood Smoked bacon, Product of Canada, 12-oz vacuum packs, with September 2026 sell-by dates. If you have it, don't eat it — return it to the store for a refund or throw it out. When in doubt, match the product name, "Product of Canada" label, size, and sell-by date against the official FSIS notice.
