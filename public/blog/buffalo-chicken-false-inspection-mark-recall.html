A year of Buffalo chicken sold under an inspection mark that was not real

USDA FSIS Class I Published September 2, 2026

On August 26, 2026, USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service announced the recall of approximately 24,900 pounds of frozen Buffalo chicken products. The reason is not a bacterium, a metal fragment or an undeclared allergen. It is that the products carried a false mark of inspection — and were produced without any federal inspection at all.

What was recalled

  • BUFFALO CHICKEN RANGOON — cardboard boxes of 100 pieces
  • BENEDETTO'S BUFFALO CHICKEN MOZZARELLA STICK — cardboard boxes of 120 pieces

Both carry "Sell By" dates ranging from July 8, 2026 to June 29, 2027, and were produced between July 8, 2025 and June 29, 2026. They were shipped by Shanghai Ravioli Corporation, of Boston, Massachusetts, to food service locations in Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Rhode Island and Vermont.

FSIS classified the recall Class I, its highest risk category. According to the agency, no confirmed illnesses have been reported.

Why a stamp matters more than it sounds

In the United States, meat and poultry cannot legally be sold across state lines unless they are produced under federal inspection. The proof is a small oval mark bearing an establishment number — here, "EST. 18004". According to FSIS, that establishment does not hold a federal grant of inspection. The number on the box did not correspond to an inspected plant.

This changes the nature of the recall. In a contamination recall, a specific hazard has been identified: Listeria in this lot, glass in that batch. Here, nothing specific has been found — and that is precisely the problem. Without inspection, there is no record of what the sanitary conditions were, whether the cooking steps were verified, whether the labelling was accurate, or whether allergens were controlled. Twelve months of production went out with no one having checked.

FSIS's Class I designation reflects that uncertainty rather than a measured contamination. The agency cannot rule out a reasonable probability of serious health consequences, because the evidence that would rule it out was never gathered.

The people who ate it never saw a box

These products went to food service — restaurants, bars, caterers — not to supermarket shelves. Buffalo chicken rangoon and Buffalo chicken mozzarella sticks arrive on a plate as an appetiser. No customer ever saw the cardboard box, the establishment number, or the sell-by date.

So the recall cannot work through consumers. It works through the operators holding the freezer stock, who are the only people able to match a box to this notice. And with sell-by dates running to June 2027, that stock can sit in a walk-in freezer for a long time: unlike fresh food, a frozen appetiser gives no signal that it has become the subject of a federal recall.

It is the same structural blind spot we described for French products sold without a label: the information exists, it is public, and it simply never reaches the person eating.

So — is this a big one?

By the numbers, no. Twenty-five thousand pounds is modest, five states is narrow, and no illnesses have been reported. It is nothing like a multistate outbreak.

By what it reveals, yes. Contamination recalls show a control system working: something was detected, traced and pulled. This one shows the opposite — a case where the guarantee itself, the mark that certifies someone looked, was not real for a year. Most food safety rests on that mark being trustworthy, including for the restaurants that bought these boxes in good faith.

What to do

If you run a restaurant, bar or catering operation in Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Rhode Island or Vermont, check your freezer for boxes labelled BUFFALO CHICKEN RANGOON (100 pieces) or BENEDETTO'S BUFFALO CHICKEN MOZZARELLA STICK (120 pieces) bearing EST. 18004. FSIS asks that these products not be served or sold: throw them away or return them to the supplier. Consumers cannot check this one themselves — if you ate Buffalo chicken appetisers in these states and feel unwell, contact a healthcare provider and mention it.
