The Cyclospora Outbreak Has Tripled: 6,358 Cases Across 15 States

CDC Cyclospora Outbreak Published August 7, 2026

Three days ago the official count stood at 1,947 illnesses in nine states. It now stands at 6,358 in fifteen. This is one of the largest foodborne parasite outbreaks the U.S. has recorded, and the numbers deserve a careful read.

Our earlier coverage: the recall that had no lot number, the Taco Bell link, and the first reported deaths.

Where it stands

  • 6,358 illnesses (was 1,947)
  • 15 states (was 9): Arkansas, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Michigan, Missouri, Nebraska, New Hampshire, North Carolina, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, West Virginia
  • 278 hospitalizations (was 98)
  • 2 deaths, in Michigan, in people with underlying health conditions
  • Last illness onset: July 31, 2026

Why a jump this large doesn't mean the lettuce is still out there

A tripling in a few days looks alarming, and the scale is genuinely serious — but it is mostly reporting catching up, not new exposure. The CDC is explicit about the lag: it can take up to six weeks to determine whether a sick person belongs to a Cyclospora outbreak. Cases reported this week are people who fell ill weeks ago.

The most reassuring number in the update is the quietest one: the last illness onset was July 31. The recalled lettuce was distributed June 29 to July 16, and Taylor Farms de México pulled all central-Mexico iceberg from the market on July 17. The onset curve stopping shortly after is what you'd expect if the recall worked.

The CDC also notes the real total is almost certainly higher than 6,358: many people recover without seeing a doctor, and Cyclospora isn't detected unless a stool test is specifically ordered for it.

What it changes for you

If you're healthy and haven't been ill, this update changes nothing practical — the implicated product was withdrawn three weeks ago. It matters if you had prolonged watery diarrhea, cramping and fatigue starting in July and never got a diagnosis. Cyclosporiasis can relapse and drag on for weeks; it responds to specific antibiotic treatment, so an unresolved case is worth naming.

What to do

If you have had weeks of watery diarrhea, cramping and fatigue since July, contact a healthcare provider and ask specifically about Cyclospora — routine stool panels don't test for it unless requested. A partial recovery is not proof you're clear, since symptoms can return. If you are immunocompromised or managing a serious condition, don't wait it out: this outbreak's two deaths were in people with underlying health conditions.
