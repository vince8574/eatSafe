When a recall has no lot numberNEW

The FDA and USDA sometimes announce a recall without publishing any lot codes — the affected items are identified only by a table of "Best if Used By" dates, or by a linked PDF.

Rather than reporting that nothing was found, Numeline flags a possible recall when the brand and product type match what you scanned, and shows you the identification details that were actually published, so you can check your own item against them.

A possible recall is never shown as a confirmed one: without a lot number, nothing proves your specific item is affected. The official notice is always one tap away.

Every profile checked, on every scan

Set up a profile for each person and Numeline tells you who a product suits and who it doesn't — here, gummy sweets flagged as possibly containing pork and gelatin for one profile, while two others are clear.

Ingredient data comes from Open Food Facts and is declarative, so it can be incomplete. Numeline flags what it finds and tells you when an origin needs checking; always read the label. A "no recall found" result means no matching recall was in the official databases at that moment — it is not a guarantee that the product is safe.
