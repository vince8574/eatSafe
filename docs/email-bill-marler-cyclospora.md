## Corps du message

```
Mr. Marler,

I run Numeline, a food recall app, and I track the CDC and FDA recall feeds daily.
A discrepancy in the Cyclospora outbreak numbers caught my attention this week and
I thought it might be worth yours.

As of the September 3 updates:

  - CDC, national:  11,458 laboratory-confirmed illnesses across 20 states,
                    at least 495 hospitalizations, 2 deaths.
  - Michigan MDHHS: 14,718 cases in Michigan alone, 366 hospitalizations,
                    cases in 72 of 83 counties plus Detroit.

One state is reporting 3,260 more illnesses than the entire national confirmed
total, and Michigan is one of twenty affected states.

Both figures appear to be correct. The CDC says on its own investigation page
that it and the FDA report only laboratory-confirmed cases, while states may
include probable illnesses and update at different frequencies. Add the six-week
lag the CDC estimates between illness onset and national reporting, and the gap
is explained without anyone being at fault.

What I keep coming back to is the consequence rather than the cause. The national
number is what sets the perceived scale of an outbreak — for press coverage, for
regulatory urgency, and for whether a physician in September thinks to order a
Cyclospora-specific stool test on a patient with six weeks of unexplained
diarrhea. A figure that is structurally a floor gets read by nearly everyone as
an estimate. In an outbreak this size, that difference is measured in untreated
people, since cyclosporiasis does not reliably clear on its own and responds to
specific therapy.

Two smaller points from the same updates, in case they are useful:

  - Georgia, Tennessee and Texas were added on August 27, after Maine and
    Massachusetts earlier in the month.
  - The last recorded illness onset moved from July 31 to August 15 — roughly a
    month after Taylor Farms de México withdrew the product on July 17.

I have no client and nothing to sell here. I wrote the discrepancy up for our
readers at numeline.com/blog, sourced entirely to the CDC and MDHHS pages, and
I am happy to share the underlying figures and links if they are of any use to
you or to Food Safety News.

With respect for your work,

Vincent Gaillard
Founder, Numeline — numeline.com
vincent@numeline.com
```
