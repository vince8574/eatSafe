## Corps du message

```
Mr. Marler,

I have read your Taylor Farms series going back to 2009, so I will not pretend
the pattern is news to you. I am writing about something narrower, in the
current numbers.

I run Numeline, a food recall app, and I read the CDC and FDA feeds daily. As of
the September 3 updates:

  - CDC, national:  11,458 laboratory-confirmed illnesses, 20 states,
                    at least 495 hospitalizations, 2 deaths.
  - Michigan MDHHS: 14,718 cases in Michigan alone, 366 hospitalizations,
                    72 of 83 counties plus Detroit.

One state reports 3,260 more illnesses than the entire national confirmed total,
and Michigan is one of twenty affected states.

Both figures are defensible. The CDC states on its investigation page that it and
the FDA report only laboratory-confirmed cases, while states may include probable
illnesses and update at different frequencies, and it estimates a six-week lag
between onset and national reporting. No one is at fault, which is what makes it
interesting.

The consequence is what stays with me. The national number sets the perceived
scale of an outbreak — for coverage, for regulatory urgency, and for whether a
physician in September thinks to order a Cyclospora-specific stool test on a
patient with six weeks of unexplained diarrhea. A figure that is structurally a
floor gets read by nearly everyone as an estimate. In an outbreak this size, that
gap is measured in people who are never diagnosed and therefore never treated.

One related detail from the same update: the last recorded illness onset moved
from July 31 to August 15 — about a month after the July 17 withdrawal.

I have no client and nothing to sell. I wrote it up for our readers, sourced only
to the CDC and MDHHS pages, and I am glad to send the underlying figures and
links if they are of any use to you or to Food Safety News.

With respect for your work,

Vincent Gaillard
Founder, Numeline — numeline.com
vincent@numeline.com
```
